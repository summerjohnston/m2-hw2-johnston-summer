Introduction

Cooking is my therapy. Food is my love language. If a friend is sick, I make them their favorite dinner. When I've had a long day, I turn on Frank Sinatra, dance around the kitchen, and make my mom's chocolate chip cookie recipe. I delight in creating new, delicious dishes.

I took multiple food media classes in my undergraduate studies, along with foundational food science and nutrition classes. Combining my love for food and writing, I became a food reporter for The Alligator, creating recipe collections and interviewing chefs around Gainesville. Actually, I almost decided to go to culinary school after graduation, but I decided that I wanted to get my master's more. Being a great cook doesn't mean you need a formal education but rather an experienced mentor and a constant hunger to learn.

My favorite movie, Ratatouille, shows the power of food to unlock nostalgia, bring people together, and propel self-discovery. Cooking is what connects me to my Greek heritage. As I experiment with spices and vegetables, I cannot shake the feeling that this is my happy place. Olive oil, honey, and sea salt are my staples in almost everything I cook. When I taste cucumbers and feta, I am transported back to my family's village on the peninsula, sharing a meal and talking loudly.

I love that cooking allows you to experiment, to create, to grow. It is a constant challenge to question how to make a traditional dish better or discover a whole new way of preparing a meal. At each step, from preparing to plating, art and science mesh together in a wonderful display of human ingenuity. There are four foundational aspects of cooking that separates good from incredible: salt, fat, acid, and heat.

The Essence of Great Cooking

Here are the elements needed for great cooking:

  • 1. Salt amplifies the flavor of quality ingredients.
  • 2. Fat carries flavor and determines whether the texture is crispy, creamy, flaky, tender, or light.
  • 3. Acid balances flavor with salt, fat, sugar, bitterness, and starch.
  • 4. Heat is where the magic happens, as a dish takes shape cooking slowly on low heat or intensely on high heat.
